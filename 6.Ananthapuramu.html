Roots & Legacy

Ananthapuramu, one of Andhra Pradesh's most historic districts, carries an economy shaped by its challenging semi-arid landscape — a story of resilience, ingenuity, and deep cultural heritage spanning millennia.

Heritage & Economy

The Working Story of Ananthapuramu

From ancient iron foundries to the granite corridors of modern India — Ananthapuramu's economic identity was forged in adversity and polished by time.

Pre-
1600s

Ancient Iron & Fort Economy

Ananthapuramu was a significant centre of the Vijayanagara Empire, whose influence shaped the region's economy for centuries. Iron smelting, fort construction, and the temple economy around Lepakshi and Tadpatri created sustained employment. Local artisans renowned for Kalamkari and stone carving contributed to temple art and trade.

Vijayanagara Heritage
1800s–
1947

Groundnut Belt & Colonial Trade

Under the Madras Presidency, Ananthapuramu became the heartland of groundnut cultivation — earning the region its enduring identity as India's peanut capital. Colonial-era rail lines to Guntakal junction made the district a key commodity transit point. Cotton and jowar supplemented the agricultural economy alongside semi-nomadic pastoralism.

Groundnut Capital of India
1950s–
1980s

Cooperative Farming & Granite Discovery

Post-independence land reforms restructured agrarian labour. The discovery of commercial granite deposits in the district's rocky terrain opened an entirely new industry. Government employment expanded through schools, irrigation projects, and administrative infrastructure. Silk weaving around Dharmavaram became a nationally acclaimed cottage industry.

Dharmavaram Silk + Granite Boom
1990s–
Present

Renewable Energy & Industrial Corridors

Ananthapuramu's harsh sun and wind became assets — it now hosts one of India's largest renewable energy hubs, with solar and wind farms creating thousands of technical and operational jobs. Cement plants, pharma units, and the Bengaluru-proximity advantage have diversified the economy substantially. The Amaravati capital region project has also stimulated construction and infrastructure employment.

Solar & Wind Energy Leader
🥜

Groundnut Capital

Ananthapuramu produces over 25% of India's groundnuts, forming the backbone of rural livelihoods and agri-processing industries.

🪨

Granite Hub

The district's Precambrian rock formations yield world-class granite, exported to Europe, China, and the Americas from dozens of quarries and processing units.

🥻

Dharmavaram Silk

The GI-tagged Dharmavaram silk sarees are celebrated across India. Thousands of weavers sustain a centuries-old tradition in organised handloom clusters.

☀️

Renewable Energy Pioneer

With over 5,000 MW of solar and wind installed capacity, Ananthapuramu is at the forefront of India's clean energy transition.

Employment Evolution

How Work Changed Here

Decade by decade, Ananthapuramu's workforce transformed from agrarian dependence to diversified industrial and service employment.

60s
1960s – 70s

Agriculture & Government Dominant

Over 80% of the workforce relied on rain-fed farming — groundnut, cotton, and jowar. Government jobs in schools and administration were the aspirational ceiling. Drought years forced seasonal migration toward Bengaluru and Chennai.

↑ Farming ↑ Govt Jobs ↓ Manufacturing
80s
1980s – 90s

Granite & Silk Industry Rise

Commercial granite quarrying expanded rapidly, employing tens of thousands in extraction, cutting, and polishing. Dharmavaram silk weaving clusters formalised. ITI and polytechnic graduates found roles in machinery and processing. Private schools multiplied.

↑ Granite ↑ Silk Weaving ↑ Private Education
00s
2000s

Cement, Pharma & Construction

Large cement plants leveraging limestone deposits created 5,000+ formal jobs. Proximity to Bengaluru drew pharma ancillaries. Infrastructure construction surged with highway projects. Retail and services expanded in Ananthapuramu city and Hindupur.

↑ Cement ↑ Construction ↑ Private Sector ↓ Subsistence Farming
20s
2010s – Present

Renewable Energy & IT Adjacency

Solar and wind farm construction and O&M roles boomed. Hindupur's proximity to Electronic City, Bengaluru enabled IT service and BPO growth. Healthcare investment accelerated. APINVEST corridors attracted logistics and warehousing operators to NH-44.

↑ Solar / Wind ↑ IT / BPO ↑ Healthcare ↓ Manual Agriculture
