Heritage & Economy

The Work History
of Bichom District

Bichom's economic roots are woven into the highlands of West and East Kameng — shaped by tribal ingenuity, forest ecology, and centuries of highland trade across the Himalayan frontier.

Pre
1900s

Tribal Subsistence Economy

The Aka (Hruso) and Miji tribes, the primary inhabitants of this region, practised jhum (slash-and-burn) cultivation, hunting, and forest gathering. Trade with Assamese plains folk introduced basic commerce — exchanging forest produce like cane, medicinal herbs, and animal hides for salt and iron tools.

1900–
1947

Colonial Frontier & Forest Economy

Under British rule, the region formed part of the North-East Frontier Agency (NEFA). Restricted from commercial exploitation, the land retained its forest cover. Timber and forest produce remained the primary economic activity. The tribal communities maintained their artisan traditions — weaving, cane craft, and indigenous jewelry-making.

1947–
1987

Post-Independence Integration

As part of the newly independent NEFA (later Arunachal Pradesh in 1972), government employment in schools, forest departments, and the military became the primary livelihoods. Road construction missions created the first formal wage-labour markets for local communities.

1987–
2023

Statehood Growth & Government Dependency

Following Arunachal Pradesh's statehood in 1987, the region saw expansion of government schemes, civil services, and welfare programmes. Horticulture (particularly kiwi, ginger, and cardamom) gained traction. The area remained part of West Kameng and East Kameng districts administratively.

2024
+

Bichom District: A New Economic Chapter

In March 2024, Bichom was officially inaugurated as Arunachal Pradesh's newest district by CM Pema Khandu. This administrative recognition has triggered investments in infrastructure, governance, and development services — opening formal employment channels in the district headquarter at Napangphung.

Employment Evolution

How Jobs Have Evolved
in Bichom

From subsistence livelihoods to structured government employment, the job landscape of Bichom reflects Arunachal's broader transition — a frontier economy opening up to the modern workforce.

Traditional Era

Forest & Farm-Based Work

The Aka and Miji communities depended on jhum cultivation, bamboo and cane harvesting, and hunting. Artisan crafts — especially weaving and basketry — were central to economic life and cultural identity.

1970s – 1990s

Rise of Government Jobs

Schoolteachers, forest guards, police personnel, and ASHA workers became the most coveted jobs. Government employment offered stability in a region with minimal private enterprise, shaping aspirations across generations.

2000s

Infrastructure & Construction Boom

Road expansion under PMGSY and BRO (Border Roads Organisation) created significant daily-wage and skilled construction employment. Contractors and local labour supervisors emerged as a new economic class.

2010s

Horticulture & MNREGS Work

Kiwi fruit, large cardamom, and ginger cultivation expanded under state horticulture missions. MNREGS (Mahatma Gandhi NREGS) guaranteed rural employment and transformed village-level incomes significantly.

2024 Onwards

District Administration & Services Sector

With Bichom now a standalone district, new government posts in administration, healthcare, education, and revenue departments are being created. Private traders, logistics operators, and small service businesses are emerging rapidly.

Future Horizon

Tourism & Eco-Economy

Bichom's biodiversity-rich forests and proximity to Pakke Tiger Reserve position it as a future eco-tourism corridor. Homestay operators, nature guides, and wildlife hospitality jobs are expected to grow strongly.
