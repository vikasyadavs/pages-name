The Economic Story of Shi Yomi

Shi Yomi, one of Arunachal Pradesh's youngest districts, was carved out of West Siang in 2018. Nestled in the breathtaking highlands along the Siyom River, the district's history is deeply woven with the traditions of the Adi tribe — skilled weavers, hunters, and agriculturalists who have sustained communities across these forested hills for centuries.

The traditional economy of Shi Yomi was built on jhum (shifting) cultivation, growing millet, rice, and maize across the river valleys. Forest produce — wild honey, medicinal herbs, bamboo, and cane — formed an equally important part of household livelihoods, traded along ancient routes connecting communities to the Brahmaputra plains.

Artisanal crafts, particularly Adi weaving with distinctive geometric patterns and the crafting of traditional tools, were central to both commerce and culture. These crafts continue to carry cultural significance even as the economic landscape transforms.

Pre-1950s
Tribal Subsistence Economy
Jhum cultivation, forest foraging, and intra-tribal barter formed the backbone of daily life for Adi communities.
1960s–80s
Government Integration Begins
NEFA administration and later Arunachal Pradesh statehood (1987) brought road connectivity, government posts, and the first formal employment opportunities.
1990s–2010s
Education & Infrastructure Expansion
Schools, healthcare centres, and roads expanded. Government services became the dominant formal employer. Hydropower potential was identified along the Siyom River.
2018–Present
District Formation & New Horizons
Shi Yomi becomes a full district with its HQ at Tato. New governance institutions, infrastructure projects, and ecotourism development open doors to fresh employment categories.
Employment Through the Decades

How Work Has Evolved in Shi Yomi

From subsistence farming to government services and now emerging eco-tourism, Shi Yomi's job market tells a story of gradual, meaningful transformation.

Pre-District Era

Agriculture & Forest Economy

Jhum cultivation, animal husbandry, and forest produce collection dominated. Work was communal, seasonal, and tied to nature's rhythms. Formal "employment" was almost nonexistent.

1980s–2000s

Government as First Employer

State government jobs in education (teachers), healthcare (ANMs, nurses), and civil services became the first formal employment. The Indian Army and ITBP also provided significant local recruitment.

2000s–2015

Infrastructure & Construction Boom

BRO (Border Roads Organisation) projects, bridge construction, and hydropower surveys created demand for skilled and semi-skilled labour. Local contractors emerged as a new class of employers.

2018–Present

District Administration & Services

New district headquarters at Tato created demand for administrative staff, clerks, data entry operators, and support services. Retail, hospitality, and small businesses started emerging in the district town.

Emerging Now

Eco-Tourism & Digital Economy

The pristine landscapes of Shi Yomi are attracting adventure tourists. Local guides, homestay operators, and content creators are finding new livelihood avenues. Mobile connectivity is enabling remote work.

On the Horizon

Hydropower & Green Energy

The Siyom River has identified hydroelectric potential. Upcoming projects are expected to bring thousands of jobs in construction, operations, and support industries to the region.
