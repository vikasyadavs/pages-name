Heritage & Roots

The Work History of Pakke-Kessang

Nestled in the foothills of the Eastern Himalayas, Pakke-Kessang has a rich economic tapestry woven from centuries of traditional practice, ecological stewardship, and community-driven enterprise.

I
Pre-Colonial Era

Agrarian Foundations & Forest Economy

Communities in the Pakke-Kessang region, comprising mainly the Nyishi and other indigenous tribes, subsisted on shifting cultivation (jhum), hunting, and gathering. The forests of the Pakke Tiger Reserve offered rich resources — bamboo, cane, medicinal herbs, and timber — forming the backbone of the local barter economy. Artisans crafted woven bamboo goods, traditional textiles, and cane furniture traded across tribal networks.

II
Colonial Period (1870s–1947)

Trade Routes & Administrative Employment

Under British administration, Pakke-Kessang's location along the foothills made it a strategic point on trade routes between the Brahmaputra plains and the highland tribal territories. Limited government postings emerged in policing, forest management, and revenue collection. Local headmen and guides were engaged as intermediaries, introducing wage-based employment to a traditionally subsistence-oriented economy.

III
Post-Independence (1947–1980)

NEFA Administration & Government Services

As part of the North-East Frontier Agency (NEFA), Pakke-Kessang saw the introduction of government schools, primary health centres, and administrative offices. Government employment became the primary aspiration for educated youth. Road construction under public works departments opened the region to the outside world, and cooperative societies for cardamom, ginger, and areca nut were established to formalise agricultural trade.

IV
Arunachal Pradesh Statehood (1987–2000)

Formalisation & District Development

Statehood brought increased Central government allocations and a surge in public sector jobs — from teaching and healthcare to forest department and police services. The establishment of the Pakke Tiger Reserve (Pakhui Wildlife Sanctuary) created new opportunities in eco-tourism, forest guard employment, and nature conservation. Local artisans began supplying handicraft markets in Itanagar and Guwahati.

V
District Formation (2018–Present)

New District, New Economic Chapter

Pakke-Kessang was carved out as a separate district in 2018, bringing dedicated administrative infrastructure, fresh government recruitment, and targeted development schemes. This catalysed demand for educated professionals across health, education, engineering, and IT. District headquarters at Pakke-Kessang town saw growth in retail, hospitality, and services — marking the region's transition into a multi-sector economy.

Employment Evolution

How Employment Has Evolved

From subsistence farming to district administration and emerging private enterprise — the employment landscape of Pakke-Kessang has transformed dramatically over decades.

🌾

Agriculture & Horticulture

Traditionally the primary livelihood, agriculture remains vital but has shifted from subsistence jhum cultivation to cash crop farming — ginger, cardamom, orange, and kiwi grown for regional markets.

▶ Transforming
🏛️

Government & Public Sector

Post-statehood, government jobs in education, police, health, PWD, and district administration have been the most sought-after. The creation of the new district in 2018 multiplied public sector openings significantly.

▲ Expanding
🦚

Forest & Eco-Tourism

The Pakke Tiger Reserve and its biodiversity — hornbills, tigers, elephants — have made eco-tourism a growing employer. Nature guides, forest guards, wildlife researchers, and lodge operators are increasingly in demand.

▲ Growing
🪨

Forestry & Extraction

Timber logging and forest product extraction, once major employers, have sharply declined following conservation mandates and the Protected Area expansion. Workers have gradually transitioned to eco-tourism and government forest service roles.

▼ Declining
🏗️

Construction & Infrastructure

Road expansion, district headquarters development, and connectivity projects under schemes like PMGSY and North-East Special Development have made construction a consistent source of semi-skilled employment for local workers.

▲ Active
📱

Private Services & Retail

Mobile connectivity and growing district headquarters have catalysed retail shops, telecom services, eateries, and private medical clinics — a nascent but rapidly expanding private sector creating entry-level to mid-level jobs.

▲ Emerging Fast
