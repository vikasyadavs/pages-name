⛰️ Heritage & Economy

The Working History of Dima Hasao

Nestled in the southern hills of Assam, Dima Hasao (formerly North Cachar Hills) has a rich and layered economic heritage shaped by its tribal communities, colonial-era infrastructure, and dense forest wealth.

The district, headquartered at Haflong — Assam's only hill station — has long been a crossroads of culture, trade, and labour. Its history is inseparable from the land it rests on: terraced paddy fields, bamboo forests, and the lifeline of the Barak valley.

⛰️ Barail Range — Dima Hasao, Assam
Pre-1900s
Tribal Agrarian Economy
The Dimasa, Hmar, Zeme Naga, and other tribal communities practised jhum (shifting cultivation), weaving, bamboo crafts, and barter trade. Community forests were the primary economic resource, sustaining livelihoods across generations.
1903 – 1947
Colonial Rail & Timber Expansion
The British established the Assam Rail Link through Haflong, spurring timber extraction and connecting the hills to lowland markets. Tea gardens began experimenting in peripheral areas. Government administrative posts became coveted employment for the educated elite.
1947 – 1972
Post-Independence Government Employment Boom
With inclusion in independent India and special provisions under the Sixth Schedule, government service became the dominant employer. Schools, health centres, and district offices proliferated, drawing educated youth into the public sector.
1972 – 2000
Autonomous Council & Development Drive
The North Cachar Hills Autonomous Council (now DHADC) was formed, channelling funds into roads, education, and healthcare. Small-scale industry and tourism began emerging around Haflong Lake and the surrounding scenic landscape.
2000 – Present
Modern Diversification & Connectivity
Infrastructure upgrades, mobile connectivity, and DONER ministry investments have opened new avenues in tourism, handicrafts, horticulture, and retail. Young people increasingly seek opportunities in Guwahati and beyond while remote work begins to bridge the gap.
📊 Employment Evolution

How Jobs Evolved in Dima Hasao

From subsistence farming to government service, and now emerging private enterprise — the job landscape of Dima Hasao reflects a district in meaningful transition.

🌾
Pre-1970s

Agriculture & Forest Economy

Jhum cultivation, forest produce collection, bamboo work, and weaving were the primary livelihoods. Work was community-based and seasonal, tied to the rhythm of the hills.

🏛️
1970s – 2000s

Government Sector Dominance

Government jobs in education, health, police, and administration became the gold standard. The DHADC and central government departments were the largest formal employers in the district.

🏗️
2000s – 2015

Infrastructure & Construction Boom

Road and bridge projects under DONER, NLCPR, and central schemes generated significant semi-skilled and skilled employment. Contractors, engineers, and labourers found multi-year work opportunities.

🛍️
2010s

Retail & Services Expansion

The growth of Haflong town as a regional hub brought retail, hospitality, and service jobs. Small businesses, hotels, and local traders began absorbing the workforce outside the government sector.

📱
2018 – Present

Digital & Tourism Opportunities

Haflong Lake tourism, homestays, and digital connectivity have created new income streams. Freelancing, content creation, and remote work are slowly taking root among the educated youth.

🚀
2025 Outlook

Emerging Sectors & Migration

Healthcare, logistics, education, and horticulture processing are the emerging bright spots. Out-migration to Guwahati, Shillong, and metros continues, but district-level opportunities are slowly catching up.
