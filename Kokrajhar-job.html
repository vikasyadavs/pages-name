Heritage & Economy

Work History
of Kokrajhar

Kokrajhar, the headquarters of the Bodoland Territorial Region (BTR), carries a rich tapestry of economic history shaped by its agrarian roots, tribal craftsmanship, and strategic location along the Brahmaputra foothills.

Pre-Colonial Era

Agrarian Bodo Communities

The indigenous Bodo people established thriving agricultural communities along the Sankosh and Manas rivers. Rice cultivation, silk weaving (Dokhona fabric), and fermented rice beer (jou) formed the economic backbone. Seasonal trade with neighboring hill communities was common.

British Colonial Period (1826–1947)

Tea Gardens & Timber Trade

The British established extensive tea gardens in the region and exploited the vast forests for timber. The region became a key railway junction — Kokrajhar station opened under the Assam Railway and Trading Company, transforming the district into a logistical hub for northeast India.

Post-Independence (1947–1990)

Government Services & Forest Economy

After independence, the district economy relied heavily on government employment, forest-based livelihoods, and subsistence farming. Small-scale rice mills, jute processing, and handloom cooperatives provided supplementary income to rural households.

BTR Formation (2003–Present)

Bodoland Territorial Region & Modern Growth

With the establishment of the Bodoland Territorial Council in 2003 and subsequent peace accords, Kokrajhar entered a new phase of governance and economic development. Infrastructure investment, educational institutions (Central University of Kokrajhar established in 2011), and emerging IT parks have transformed the employment landscape.

Employment Through Decades

How Jobs Evolved
in Kokrajhar

1950s – 1970s

Forest & Farm Era

Employment was almost entirely agrarian. The Forest Department was the largest employer alongside subsistence farming. Women's primary work was domestic and handloom-based, producing the iconic Bodo Dokhona and Aronai textiles for local trade.

Agriculture dominant
1980s – 1990s

Public Sector Expansion

Government employment surged as schools, district offices, railways, and healthcare centres expanded. However, ethnic tensions and the Bodo movement caused economic disruption and displacement, limiting private sector growth significantly during this period.

Govt. services grew
2000s – Present

Peace, Education & New Economy

The 2003 Bodoland Accord brought stability. Central University of Kokrajhar (2011), IIIT Kokrajhar (2013), and BTR infrastructure investments attracted educated youth back. IT, healthcare, FMCG logistics, and tourism sectors have begun emerging as meaningful employers.

Diversification underway
