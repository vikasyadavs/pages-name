Heritage & Economy

The Work History of Valsad

A coastal gem in South Gujarat, Valsad has been a seat of trade, agriculture, and artisanship for centuries. Here's how its economy took shape.

Pre-colonial Era

Agricultural Roots & Coastal Trade

Valsad (historically known as Bulsar) thrived as an agricultural belt cultivating paddy, sugarcane, and vegetables. Its coastline facilitated sea trade with Arabia and East Africa, making it a minor but active port town under the Gujarat Sultanate and later the Maratha Confederacy.

British Raj (1800s–1947)

Railway Connectivity & Textile Emergence

The arrival of the Western Railway in the mid-19th century transformed Valsad into a vital transit node. Small-scale weaving and cotton ginning units emerged. The region also became known for its chikoo (sapodilla) orchards, which remain a signature crop to this day.

Post-Independence (1947–1980s)

Industrial Awakening

With Gujarat's industrialization drive, Valsad attracted small-scale manufacturing units in chemicals, dyes, and plastics. The establishment of industrial estates at Vapi (in proximity to Valsad) began pulling labor from the district into factory employment for the first time at scale.

1990s–2010s

Diversification & IT Influence

Economic liberalisation opened Valsad to services, retail banking, and emerging IT roles. Many educated youth began commuting to Surat and Mumbai for professional careers, while the district itself saw growth in education institutions, healthcare facilities, and retail trade.

🌾 Agriculture & Horticulture

Valsad is among Gujarat's most productive districts for horticulture. It is celebrated as the "Chikoo Capital of India", contributing a large share of India's sapodilla production. Mango, chickoo, and coconut plantations employ thousands of farmhands and agri-entrepreneurs.

Rice and paddy cultivation continue in fertile valleys, supplemented by dairy farming in rural talukas like Dharampur and Kaprada.

🏭 Artisans & Craftspeople

Tribal communities in Dharampur and Kaprada talukas have preserved centuries-old craft traditions — warli-inspired art, bamboo weaving, and handloom textiles. Government-supported self-help groups (SHGs) now help these artisans market their products nationally and abroad.

The district's craft heritage has increasingly found markets through e-commerce platforms, opening new income avenues for rural families.

Employment Evolution

How Jobs Evolved in Valsad

From subsistence farming to manufacturing corridors — Valsad's employment landscape has undergone a remarkable transformation over five decades.

1950s – 1970s

Agriculture Dominates

Over 70% of the working population was engaged in farming, forestry, and allied activities. Government jobs — primarily in education and public administration — were considered the most prestigious form of employment. Women's labour force participation was largely informal and agrarian.

1980s – 1990s

Industrial Migration Begins

The Vapi-Silvassa industrial corridor lured thousands from rural Valsad into chemical plants, textile mills, and packaging factories. This era saw the rise of the daily wage worker. Private sector employment began overtaking government roles for the first time, particularly among male youth.

2000s – 2010s

Services & Education Sector Surge

New schools, colleges, and hospitals created thousands of teaching, nursing, and administrative roles. The BFSI (Banking, Financial Services, Insurance) sector expanded through rural branch openings. Retail chains entered the district's main towns, creating organized retail employment for the first time.

2015 – Present

Digital Economy & Skilled Trades

IT and digital services roles — data entry, BPO, web development, digital marketing — have grown rapidly, often filled by youth educated in Surat but returning home. The government's skill development programs (PMKVY, DDU-GKY) have formalized trade skills like welding, electrical work, and beautician services, enabling local employment.
