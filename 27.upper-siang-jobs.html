Work Heritage

The Economic Roots
of Upper Siang

From ancient trade routes along the Siang river to the forests and highlands that shaped self-sufficient communities — Upper Siang has always been a land of purposeful work.

Pre-Colonial Era
River Trade & Forest Economies
The Adi tribes of Upper Siang engaged in subsistence farming, hunting, and vibrant barter trade along the Siang (Brahmaputra) river corridor. Cardamom, cane, bamboo, and forest produce were traded with neighbouring communities. The region's dense biodiversity sustained both livelihood and culture.
Early 20th Century
British Administration & Road Access
Limited British frontier administration introduced the first structured land use patterns. Yingkiong emerged as a nascent administrative centre. The region remained largely self-reliant with no significant industrial inroad, preserving its agrarian and forest-based economy through remote geography.
1950s – 1970s
Integration into Independent India
Post-independence, NEFA (North-East Frontier Agency) programs brought the first government employment — forest departments, school teachers, and road workers. The district headquarters at Yingkiong began providing structured public-sector roles. Agriculture diversified into wet rice cultivation, millet, and maize farming.
1980s – 1990s
Artisan Crafts & Handloom Growth
Adi weavers gained recognition for their distinctive cane-and-bamboo crafts and hand-woven textiles. Women-led cottage industries in bamboo handicrafts and tribal jewellery formed a significant income stream. Government cooperatives supported the marketing of these products across Arunachal Pradesh.
2000s – Present
Hydropower & Infrastructure Boom
Upper Siang's steep river terrain identified it as one of India's premier hydropower corridors. Large infrastructure projects brought engineering, civil, and technical employment. Road connectivity under PMGSY and BRO opened markets and enabled commerce at an unprecedented scale for the district.
Employment Evolution

How Work Has Changed
in Upper Siang

Decades of policy, infrastructure, and migration have redrawn the employment map of the district — from purely agrarian to a hybrid of government, construction, and emerging private enterprise.

Sectors That Contracted
Traditional Subsistence Work
Jhum (shifting) cultivation, once the primary livelihood for most households, has declined significantly as younger generations migrate to towns for education and employment. Forest-based foraging and artisan crafts also saw reduced participation due to market access challenges and urbanisation pressure.
Sectors That Expanded
Government & Infrastructure Jobs
State government positions — teachers, healthcare workers, district administration — remain the most sought-after employment category. Since 2010, hydropower project construction has generated thousands of temporary and skilled contract roles. BRO road projects and NHIDCL highway development also provide consistent employment to local workers.
Emerging Categories
Tourism, Tech & Services
Eco-tourism and adventure tourism are creating new hospitality roles — guides, homestay operators, and trekking support staff. A small but growing services sector has emerged in Yingkiong with retail, transportation, and telecom employment. Educated youth are increasingly entering remote-work roles through internet connectivity improvements.
Structural Shift
Private vs Public Balance
Government employment still constitutes the majority of formal jobs in Upper Siang. The private formal sector remains underdeveloped, with most private employment in construction contracts, small trade, and transport. NGOs and development organisations also provide employment to educated local professionals in community welfare roles.
