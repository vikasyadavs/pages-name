About the District

The Heart of
Barak Valley

Karimganj is a district of southern Assam, cradled between the Barak River and the lush hills of the Indo-Bangladesh borderlands. Formerly part of undivided Cachar, it became a separate district in 1983.

Blessed with abundant rainfall, tropical forests, and fertile plains, the district is renowned for its tea gardens, bamboo groves, and vibrant multi-cultural communities including Bengalis, Bishnupriyas, Manipuris and tribal peoples.

Living Traditions

Culture &
Festivals

Eid-ul-Fitr

Celebrated with great fervour by the sizable Muslim community with prayers, feasts and communal gatherings.

Durga Puja

Grandly observed by the Bengali Hindu community with elaborate pandals, cultural programmes and processions.

Bihu

The Assamese harvest festival celebrated with folk music, bihu dance and community feasts across the district.

Manipuri Raas Leela

Sacred dance-dramas performed by the Bishnupriya Manipuri community — a UNESCO-recognised living tradition.

A Mosaic of Communities

Karimganj is a remarkable confluence of cultures. Bengalis, Bishnupriya Manipuris, Assamese, Dimasa, Hmar, and Muslim communities have coexisted for centuries, creating a unique syncretic identity.

The district's cuisine reflects this diversity — from pungent shutki maach to delicate manipuri eromba, from pitha sweets to biryani. The local markets are a sensory festival of spices, handlooms, and fresh produce.

Handloom weaving is a proud craft heritage, with local weavers producing exquisite mekhela chadors, manipuri stoles, and tribal textiles that carry centuries of artistic tradition.
