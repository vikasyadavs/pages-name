Our Heritage

The Rich Legacy of Sivasagar

Once the imperial capital of the Ahom Kingdom, Sivasagar carries six centuries of regal history — from grand temples to the world's first oil wells.

🏯 Ahom Kingdom — The Imperial Capital

Sivasagar, historically known as Rangpur, served as the capital of the Ahom Kingdom for nearly 600 years (1253–1826 CE). The Ahoms built remarkable monuments — the Sivadol, Vishnudol, and Karenghar palace — that still stand as testimony to an advanced civilization. This royal legacy forged a deeply artisanal and administrative economy supported by skilled craftspeople, temple priests, royal weavers, and military commanders.

🛢️ Birthplace of the Oil Industry

Sivasagar holds a unique distinction: Digboi, located in its vicinity, is home to Asia's first oil refinery (est. 1901). The discovery of petroleum transformed the district's economic character. Oil exploration brought railways, technical expertise, and a new class of industrial laborers — making Sivasagar a pioneer in India's industrial history long before independence.

🌾 Agriculture & Weaving Traditions

Beyond oil and monuments, Sivasagar's fertile valleys nourished rice cultivation, mustard farming, and sericulture. The Mising and other indigenous communities practiced generations-old traditions of Eri and Pat silk weaving, while seasonal river fisheries along the Disang and Dikhow rivers sustained thousands of families.

The Workforce

Employment Evolution

From paddy fields and royal courts to oil rigs and private offices — how livelihoods in Sivasagar have transformed across four generations.

Pre-1900s

Royal Economy & Agrarian Society

During the Ahom era, employment revolved around the royal court, temple administration, and agriculture. Skilled craftspeople built monuments and maintained them. The ryot (peasant farmer) system supported rice and mustard cultivation, while specialized guilds of weavers, potters, and metal workers supplied the royal household and trade routes leading into Burma.

1900s – 1947

Oil Discovery & Colonial Industry

The discovery of oil near Digboi at the turn of the 20th century fundamentally altered Sivasagar's workforce. British-built refineries, railways, and administrative outposts created the district's first industrial labour class. Tea estates on the district's fringes employed tens of thousands of migrant workers. Colonial government offices drew educated Assamese into clerical and administrative roles for the first time.

1947 – 2000

Public Sector Dominance

Post-independence, ONGC operations expanded significantly, becoming the district's single largest employer. Government schools, hospitals, and the district administration absorbed the growing educated workforce. Agriculture modernized with irrigation projects but its employment share declined. Oil and government jobs became the twin aspirations of every Sivasagar household.

2000s – Present

Diversification & Heritage Tourism Boom

The 21st century has seen Sivasagar diversify beyond oil. Heritage tourism around Ahom monuments brings hospitality and guide employment. Private schools, nursing homes, retail chains, and small manufacturing units have emerged. Digital connectivity has enabled remote work and e-commerce logistics. The district is now attracting investors through Assam's industrial and tourism development schemes.
