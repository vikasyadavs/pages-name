Work Heritage

The Economic Roots of Hojai

Hojai district, nestled in the fertile Kapili-Jamuna valley of Central Assam, carries a rich legacy of trade, agriculture, and craftsmanship dating back to ancient kingdoms. The region, once part of the Davaka kingdom (up to the 6th century CE), was a crossroads of commerce in eastern India.

The economy was historically anchored in agriculture — paddy cultivation thrived in the alluvial plains, while the surrounding forests supplied timber and medicinal herbs. Traditional artisans crafted bamboo goods, handloom textiles, and cane products that were traded across the Brahmaputra valley.

After the Sylhet Referendum of 1947, a large wave of Bengali Hindu refugees settled in towns like Lumding, Lanka, and Hojai, bringing with them a vibrant entrepreneurial culture that transformed local trade and small businesses. This demographic shift reshaped the district's commercial character for generations.

Lumding, one of Hojai's major towns, became a critical railway junction under British rule — a status that made it a logistical hub and boosted its working-class employment base significantly.

6C
Up to 6th Century CE
The Davaka Kingdom Era

The Kapili Valley was home to the independent Davaka Kingdom. Agriculture, forest produce, and river trade formed the economic backbone of the region.

18C
18th Century
Ahom & Kachari Influence

Under Ahom and Kachari kingdoms, the region saw organized paddy farming and weaving. Traditional handloom became a household industry passed through generations.

BR
British Raj
Railway Junction & Colonial Trade

Lumding emerged as a major railway junction under colonial rule, fueling employment in railways, tea plantations, and organized timber trade across the Nowgong district.

47
Post-1947
Refugee Resettlement & Trade Boom

Bengali Hindu migrants from Sylhet brought entrepreneurial energy, establishing flourishing markets and businesses in Hojai and Lanka towns, diversifying the local economy.

15
2015 Onwards
District Formation & Modern Growth

Hojai became a separate district on 15 August 2015, sparking new government investment in infrastructure, healthcare, and education — opening formal employment channels.

Employment Evolution

How Work Has Changed in Hojai

From subsistence farming to railways, from textile mills to digital services — Hojai's workforce has navigated remarkable transitions over the decades.

🌾
Pre-Independence
Agricultural Dominance

Over 80% of the population depended on paddy cultivation, jute, and fishing in the Kapili-Jamuna floodplains. Subsistence farming dominated with minimal formal employment.

⬛ Subsistence Era
🚂
1950s – 1970s
Railways & Government Jobs

Lumding Railway Junction became a major employment hub. Government service — railways, state departments, schools — emerged as the most coveted career paths in the district.

▲ Growing
🏭
1980s – 1990s
Small Industry & Textiles

Small-scale industries, handloom cooperatives, and agro-processing units absorbed a growing workforce. The Lanka and Hojai markets became regional trade centres for goods.

▲ Expanding
🏗️
2000s – 2010s
Infrastructure & Service Sector

Road construction, healthcare expansion, and private retail opened new jobs. MNREGA and government schemes formalized rural employment for the first time at scale.

▲ Diversifying
💻
2015 – Present
District Status & Digital Growth

New district status in 2015 (reinstated 2023) triggered administrative expansion. E-commerce, mobile retail, logistics, and education tech are now creating first-generation white-collar jobs.

▲ Rapid Growth
📉
Declining Sectors
Handloom & Traditional Crafts

Traditional handloom and cane-bamboo crafts have faced stiff competition from mass-produced goods, leading to a gradual decline in artisan employment — though revival efforts are underway.

▼ Declining

Top Employers

Notable Companies in Hojai Region

These organisations are among the leading employers operating in and around Hojai district, spanning government, industry, healthcare, and services.

NE
Northeast Frontier Railway (NFR)
Railways & Logistics

Lumding Division of NFR is one of Hojai's largest employers, operating the strategically vital railway junction with hundreds of staff across technical and administrative roles.

AS
Assam State Transport Corporation
Public Transport

ASTC operates bus services through Hojai, providing employment to drivers, mechanics, and administrative staff serving the Central Assam corridor.

HB
Hojai Bazaar Traders Association
Retail & Trade

The thriving Hojai market is one of Assam's busiest wholesale trading hubs, employing thousands in retail, wholesale, and warehousing operations year-round.

DH
District Hospital Hojai
Government Healthcare

The state-run district hospital is a key healthcare employer with medical officers, nurses, paramedics, and support staff serving over 9 lakh residents across three tehsils.

KF
Kapili Valley Foods Pvt. Ltd.
Agro-Processing

A local agro-processing unit dealing in rice milling, mustard oil extraction, and packaged food products, providing direct and indirect employment across the rural economy.

LK
Lanka Industrial Estate
Small Scale Industry

The Lanka SIDBI-supported industrial cluster houses multiple small manufacturers in plastics, textiles, and engineering goods — a growing centre for blue-collar employment.

SB
State Bank of India – Hojai Branch
Banking & Finance

SBI and its associate rural banks operate multiple branches across Hojai, Lanka, and Doboka, employing banking officers, clerks, and customer service executives.

RE
Reliance Retail (Hojai Outlet)
Organized Retail

The organised retail wave has reached Hojai, with outlets like Reliance Retail offering entry-level employment in sales, inventory, and customer relationship management.

SP
Sankardev Poly Clinic
Private Healthcare

A prominent private healthcare provider in Sankardev Nagar (district HQ), offering jobs for specialists, nurses, diagnostic technicians, and hospital admin staff.

DE
Doboka Educational Trust
Education

Managing several schools and colleges across Doboka tehsil, this trust is among the larger educational employers, offering teaching and administrative roles in rural Hojai.
