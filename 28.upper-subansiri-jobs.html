Work Heritage

The Economic Roots of
Upper Subansiri

From the lush valleys along the Subansiri river to the ancient trade networks of the Apatani, Nyishi, and Hill Miri communities — Upper Subansiri's economy has always been shaped by the land and its people.

Pre-Colonial Era
Tribal Agronomy & River Trade
The Apatani people of Ziro, the administrative centre of Lower Subansiri, and the broader Nyishi community of Upper Subansiri practised highly evolved wet rice cultivation and forest farming. Barter exchange of rice, forest produce, mithun cattle, and handwoven textiles formed the economic foundation. Seasonal trade routes through mountain passes connected the district to Assam's plains.
Early 20th Century
NEFA Frontier & Forest Economy
As part of the North-East Frontier Agency, Upper Subansiri remained largely insulated from colonial commerce. Livelihood centred on jhum cultivation, mithun rearing, fishing in the Subansiri and its tributaries, and skilled bamboo-cane craftsmanship. Women's weaving traditions produced distinctive textiles that were both functional and ceremonial.
1960s – 1980s
Government Administration & First Roads
Post-independence integration brought structured public employment to Daporijo — the district headquarters. Forest department staff, government schoolteachers, and medical workers constituted the first formal wage earners. Road construction under NEFA welfare programs opened connectivity, gradually enabling modest commercial activity in the headquarter town.
1990s – 2000s
Handicrafts, Horticulture & Cooperative Growth
State government horticulture programs promoted kiwi, orange, pineapple, and ginger farming across the Subansiri basin. Women's cooperatives in tribal weaving received national recognition, connecting Nyishi textiles to crafts markets beyond the state. Small-scale trader settlements grew around Daporijo as access roads improved.
2010s – Present
Hydropower, Tourism & Digital Connectivity
The Subansiri Lower Hydroelectric Project (SLHEP) — among India's largest run-of-river projects — brought significant infrastructure and engineering employment to the region. Concurrent BRO road expansion under the Trans-Arunachal Highway project created large-scale construction employment. Emerging eco-tourism and internet connectivity are now opening new livelihood channels for the district's educated youth.
Employment Evolution

How Work Has Changed
in Upper Subansiri

Decades of policy change, infrastructure investment, and migration have redrawn the district's employment map — from subsistence livelihoods to a mixed economy with government, construction, and growing private enterprise.

📉
Sectors That Contracted
Jhum (shifting) cultivation has seen sharp decline as younger populations move to Daporijo and Itanagar for education and employment. Traditional bamboo and cane cottage industries lost market share due to machine-made alternatives. Forest-based foraging has been curtailed by conservation regulations and livelihood shifts.
📈
Sectors That Expanded
State government roles — teachers, nurses, police, and administrative staff — remain the most aspirational employment category. The Subansiri hydropower project injected civil engineering, machine operations, and project management roles. Road construction under BRO and NHIDCL provides consistent, large-scale labourer and technician employment.
🌱
Emerging Opportunities
Eco-tourism and adventure tourism along the Subansiri river are creating hospitality, guiding, and logistics roles. Digital infrastructure expansion has enabled remote work adoption among educated youth. NGO and development organisation roles in tribal welfare, environment, and women's empowerment are growing in the district.
⚖️
Government vs Private Balance
Formal employment is still dominated by government jobs at over 70% of organised sector roles. The private formal sector is nascent — mostly construction contractors, transporters, and small retail. The informal economy — daily wage labour, seasonal farming, and petty trade — remains a significant parallel livelihood system across the district.
