Heritage & Economy

The Work History of
Karbi Anglong

From ancient tribal craftsmanship in the Mikir Hills to the bustling market towns of Diphu — a story of land, community, and quiet endurance.

Ancient

Karbi Tribal Economy & Forest Livelihoods

The Karbi people (also known as Mikirs) have inhabited these hills for centuries, developing a rich self-sufficient economy rooted in jhum (shifting) cultivation, forest produce gathering, and artisan traditions. Handloom weaving, bamboo craft, wood carving, and the iconic Karbi textile patterns formed the economic foundation. Trade in forest goods — honey, resin, medicinal herbs, and timber — connected the hills to the Brahmaputra valley markets.

Colonial

British Administration & Forest Economy

Under British rule, Karbi Anglong (then part of the Mikir Hills district) was recognised for its rich timber and mineral reserves. Teak and hardwood logging became a significant economic activity, with the colonial administration using the region's forests for railway sleepers and construction. Tea gardens on the foothills fringes provided wage labour. The region was administered as a partially excluded area, limiting commercial penetration but preserving traditional livelihoods.

1960s

Post-Independence & District Formation

Karbi Anglong district was formed in 1951, and the establishment of Diphu as the district headquarters kickstarted organised commerce. Government schools, health centres, and district offices created the first significant formal employment opportunities. Agriculture — particularly paddy, mustard, and ginger — remained the primary livelihood. Mining of coal, limestone, and oil in pockets of the district attracted early industrial interest.

1980s–90s

Autonomous Council & Infrastructure Growth

The creation of the Karbi Anglong Autonomous Council (1976) devolved significant governance, spurring road infrastructure and public sector employment. Diphu and Bokajan emerged as trade and market centres. The Bokajan Cement Plant became a major industrial employer. Road connectivity improvements gradually opened the hills to broader economic participation, while government employment became the dominant aspiration for educated youth.

Evolution of Employment

How Work Has Evolved
in Karbi Anglong

From jhum fields and timber yards to autonomous council offices and agri-processing units — five decades of change.

1970s
Public Sector as Anchor

Government was the primary employer — forest department, district administration, schools, and newly created autonomous council offices. Sarkari naukri was the defining aspiration; a government job meant stability for an entire family across generations.

Forest DeptAdminEducation
1980s
Industry & Trade Take Root

Bokajan Cement Plant operations expanded, becoming the region's largest industrial employer. Diphu's weekly markets grew into permanent commercial hubs. Small-scale mining of coal and limestone in Langhin and Lumbajong areas offered informal employment to thousands.

CementMiningTrade
1990s
Road Connectivity Opens Markets

NH-36 and improved road networks connected Karbi Anglong to Guwahati and Nagaon, enabling agriculture and forest produce to reach wider markets. Private trading firms, medical shops, and construction work multiplied. Youth migration to Guwahati for education and employment began in earnest.

AgricultureTransportConstruction
2000s
Healthcare & Education Boom

Private hospitals, nursing homes, and colleges established in Diphu created hundreds of skilled-sector jobs. Peace agreements after period of insurgency opened the region to investment. Tourism in Kaziranga's buffer zones and eco-tourism in the hills emerged as new opportunity sectors.

HealthcareEducationTourism
2010s
Agri-Processing & Connectivity

Ginger, pineapple, and turmeric processing units emerged, adding value to Karbi Anglong's rich agricultural output. National Highway upgrades and railway expansion towards Lumding improved logistics. Government schemes like PMGSY transformed rural access, spawning new rural economy jobs.

Agri-ProcessingLogisticsRural Dev
2020s
Digital & Green Economy

Mobile internet penetration brought e-commerce delivery, digital services, and remote work possibilities. Bamboo and handloom enterprises supported by KAAC funding created women's SHG employment. Renewable energy projects in the hills opened engineering and operations job streams.

E-CommerceHandicraftsGreen Energy
