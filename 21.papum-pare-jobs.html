Heritage & Economy

The Work History of Papum Pare

Nestled along the foothills of the Eastern Himalayas, Papum Pare has a proud tradition of self-reliance — from tribal craftsmanship to modern governance.

Pre-Independence Era
🌿 Tribal Agriculture & Artisan Traditions
Communities of the Nyishi, Galo, and other tribes practiced shifting cultivation (jhum farming), weaving, cane-and-bamboo craftsmanship, and traded forest produce across highland passes. The Papum River valley formed the lifeline of early subsistence economies.
🌾
🏛️
1960s – 1970s
🏛️ NEFA Administration & Government Entry
With the reorganization of the North-East Frontier Agency (NEFA), Papum Pare saw its first wave of formal employment through government posts. Itanagar emerged as the administrative nucleus, drawing clerical and civil service workers into structured employment.
1980s – 1990s
🏗️ Statehood & Infrastructure Boom
Arunachal Pradesh's statehood in 1987 brought a surge of public works — roads, bridges, and public buildings. Papum Pare, home to Itanagar (the state capital), became the epicentre of government employment. Construction and trade flourished as the capital city grew.
🏗️
⚡
2000s – 2010s
⚡ Hydropower, Trade & Education Expansion
The Ranganadi hydro project and other energy ventures created skilled-labour demand. Private schools, colleges, and coaching institutes proliferated. Cross-border trade with neighbouring states brought retail and logistics jobs to Naharlagun and Nirjuli townships.
2020s – Present
💡 Digital Economy & Diverse Workforce
Today, Papum Pare is embracing digital services, e-governance, healthcare, and tourism. Startups, government schemes like PMEGP, and the National Games legacy infrastructure have created a diverse, aspirational job market across public and private sectors.
💡
Employment Evolution

How Employment Evolved in Papum Pare

From jhum fields to government offices to digital desks — the working life of Papum Pare tells a story of remarkable transformation across generations.

60s
1960s
Civil Service Pioneers
The first educated generation entered government posts as NEFA administration expanded. Teaching, forest department, and police services were the most sought-after roles. Formal employment was limited but prestigious.
80s
1980s
Statehood Surge
Hundreds of new government posts were created after statehood. Secretariat jobs, PWD roles, and education department positions dominated. Private entrepreneurship was minimal; government was the primary employer.
90s
1990s
Trade & Commerce Emergence
Naharlagun's market expanded as traders from Assam and beyond set up shop. Petty trade, transport, and the hospitality sector started providing non-government livelihoods for the first time at scale.
00s
2000s
Education & Healthcare Boom
Private schools, coaching institutes, clinics, and nursing homes proliferated. Teaching and healthcare became key private-sector employers. APPSC and APSSB exams saw skyrocketing applicants for government roles.
10s
2010s
Infrastructure & Hydro Projects
Large-scale hydropower and road projects brought thousands of skilled and unskilled contract jobs. The capital district attracted engineers, technicians, and managers from across India, changing the labour market dynamics significantly.
20s
2020s
Digital & Gig Economy
E-commerce, freelancing, digital marketing, and remote work have emerged. The National Games 2025 infrastructure has spurred hospitality and tourism employment. Young graduates now actively explore private and startup opportunities beyond government jobs.
